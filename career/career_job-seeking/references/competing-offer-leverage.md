# Competing Offer Leverage Strategy

## When to Use
Maxime has a real offer from Company A and wants to accelerate Company B's process to make a decision before A's deadline.

## The Escalation Ladder

### Tier 1: HR/Recruiter (First Move)
Send a polite email to the HR contact at Company B:
- Express interest and preference for B
- Disclose that you have an offer from A (genuine only — never fabricate)
- Ask for accelerated timeline / next steps

**Email structure:**
1. Update on your candidacy ("I've completed [stage] and am very interested")
2. Disclosure ("I've received an offer from [Company A]")
3. Assurance ("You are my top choice")
4. Ask ("Can we accelerate next steps?")

**Example (from real use — Intersect/Tonya, May 2026):**
> "I am writing to share an update regarding my candidacy. I have just received an offer at CAISO. While the CAISO offer is satisfactory, I remain very excited about the [Role] at [Company B]. Given my interest in [specific reason] and my regard for the team, [Company B] remains my top choice. I informed [Company A] that I am interviewing with another company and requested more time. Would it be possible to provide an accelerated timeline?"

**Timing:** Send immediately when you have an offer in hand.
**Expected response:** 1-3 business days. If no acknowledgment in >1.5 days, escalate.

### Tier 2: Internal Advocate (Highest Leverage)
The person who referred you or a colleague at Company B. This is the fastest channel — they can walk to the HM's desk / Slack them directly.

**Prerequisites:**
- You have a genuine relationship (not a cold referral)
- The advocate has context (they know your background, the role, the team)
- You have a concrete deadline to share

**Message structure:**
1. Status update — "I passed the [HR screen / technical round] with [name]"
2. Deadline — "I received an offer from [Company A] with a deadline of [date]. I can probably buy [X] more days by negotiating."
3. Clear ask — "Can you check with [HM name] where things stand and whether we can accelerate the technical rounds?"

**Example (from real use — Craig/Intersect, May 2026):**
> "Quick update: I passed the HR screen with Tonya and I'm very keen on the validation engineer role. Complicating factor: I received an offer from CAISO with a hard deadline Monday. I can probably buy a couple extra days by negotiating, but I'm in a time bind. Intersect is absolutely my top choice. Is there any chance you could check with Ed on where things stand?"

**Key rule:** Include ALL three elements (status, deadline, ask). Don't assume the advocate knows where you are in the process.

### Tier 3: Hiring Manager (Direct)
Skip HR entirely and go to the person who makes hiring decisions. Use when:
- HR is unresponsive
- The internal advocate isn't available
- Time is critically short

**Approach:** Short email — same structure as Tier 1 but addressed to the HM. Mention you've been through whatever screening exists and are in active process.

## Timeline Management

### Buying Time on the Existing Offer
- **Ask for more money** — This is the most natural reason to delay. "I'd like to consider your offer, but I'm also in late-stage discussions with another company. Can I have until [date] to respond?"
- **Contract roles are more flexible** — Staffing agencies often have looser deadlines than FTE roles.
- **Don't overplay** — 1 week extension is reasonable. 2+ weeks may look like you're fishing.

### Clock Management
| Day | Action |
|-----|--------|
| 0 | Receive offer A. Send Tier 1 email to Company B HR. |
| 1-1.5 | Wait. Check email. No response yet = normal. |
| 1.5-2 | No acknowledgment from HR? Send Tier 2 to internal advocate. |
| 2-3 | Advocate responds? Good. If advocate also can't get traction, consider Tier 3 to HM. |
| Deadline - 1 | Make decision. Accept A or decline. Do not burn bridges at B — "I accepted another offer but would love to stay in touch." |

## Pitfalls

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| HR feels pressured → drops your candidacy | Frame as "I want to choose you, help me" not "I have a better offer, match it" |
| Internal advocate is too busy to help | Keep it short. Make the ask a single sentence they can forward to the HM. |
| Company B can't accelerate | Accept that some processes are fixed. Prepare to choose or decline A. |
| Losing both offers | Real risk. Don't bluff — only use this strategy with a real offer in hand. |
| Advocate changes companies / role | Verify current employment before sending. If they've left Company B, they can't help. |
