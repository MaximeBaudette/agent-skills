# Interview Benefits Questions — Framing Guide

## General Principle
Ask about benefits to understand **how the company operates and supports its engineers** — not to shop for perks. Frame every benefits question as a question about culture, workflow, or professional growth.

## Good Framing vs. Bad Framing

| ❌ Benefits-shopper framing | ✅ Professional framing |
|---|---|
| "What's the PTO policy?" | "How does the team handle work-life balance and time off — is there a minimum PTO expectation or is it truly flexible?" |
| "Do you offer remote work stipends?" | "I noticed the company offers home office support — how does the team typically set up their remote work environment?" |
| "What's the 401k match?" | "How does the company think about total compensation and long-term financial wellness for employees?" |
| "Tell me about parental leave." | "How does the team support employees through major life events like parental leave?" |
| "What learning budget do you have?" | "How does the company invest in professional development — are there conferences, training budgets, or internal learning programs?" |

## When to Ask About Benefits

### HR Screen (First Round)
- **OK:** Brief questions about remote work culture, team setup, professional development
- **Avoid:** Salary negotiation, detailed benefits breakdown, PTO specifics
- **Good question:** "How does the team coordinate across locations day-to-day?"

### Later Rounds (Hiring Manager, Panel)
- **OK:** Questions about learning budget, team growth, engineering culture
- **Good question:** "What does professional growth look like on this team — is there budget for conferences or training?"

### Offer Stage
- **Full discussion appropriate:** Salary, equity, benefits, PTO, start date — this is the right time for details

## Key Topics to Ask About (Professional Frame)

1. **Remote/culture:** "How does the team stay connected across locations?"
2. **Growth:** "How does the company invest in employee development?"
3. **Work-life:** "How does the team think about sustainable pace and avoiding burnout?"
4. **Comp philosophy:** "How does the company approach total compensation — base, equity, and benefits together?"

## Never Ask
- "What's the maximum PTO I can take?"
- "How many sick days do I get?"
- Anything that signals you're optimizing for benefits rather than the work itself
- Questions whose answers are clearly on the company website or in the offer letter
